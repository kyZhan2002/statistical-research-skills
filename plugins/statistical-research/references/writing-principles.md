# Shared statistical writing principles

Read this reference whenever using any of the four statistical research skills. Apply it to authored prose, explanations, captions, and slide text. The invoking skill determines the deliverable and level of detail. Preserve mathematical meaning, exact quotations, bibliographic records, and executable syntax.

## Central message and supported contributions

Identify the statistical question, the main advance, and the evidence supporting it before writing. Connect the selected results into a coherent story and explain their consequences. Use clear, concise interpretation after important results when helpful, rather than merely restating formulas. State contributions strongly through precise, supported claims.

In an abstract, introduction, or opening presentation frames, establish the relevant background and why the problem matters before introducing technical machinery. Keep abstracts high-level with minimal notation. In an introduction's contribution discussion, use concise formal definitions when they clarify the advance, and state a rate when it is a main contribution. Explain what the notation or rate means and retain only the formal detail needed to make the contribution precise. Usually reserve assumptions for the methods or theory section, while giving a brief scope qualification or cross-reference when needed for accuracy. Keep background specific to the problem rather than adding generic claims of importance. For a local proof or simulation section, provide only the context needed for that section instead of repeating the paper's opening.

Frame contributions and covered regimes constructively: explain what is established, under which conditions, and how it advances or complements prior work. Replace defensive positioning such as "cannot do X", "is not doing X", or "is limited by X" with an accurate description of the target and guarantee. Retain material qualifications, counterexamples, and established impossibility results. In an audit, report errors and failed claims directly. Constructive framing governs exposition, not the mathematical verdict.

Compare like quantities under comparable assumptions and identify the particular advance over cited work. Verify citations and novelty claims. Distinguish proofs, empirical findings, intuition, and conjectures. Never invent facts or strengthen claims merely to improve their presentation.

## Economical notation and proportionate explanation

Introduce notation only when necessary or when it materially simplifies repeated reasoning. Define every new symbol at first use, or in an earlier, clearly identified notation paragraph, subsection, or slide. Explain its purpose when useful. Group essential related objects with a compact account of their roles. Preserve established symbols and macros. A later glossary does not excuse an undefined first use.

Allocate explanation to the consequential ideas and difficult transitions. In proofs, give decisive arguments and uses of assumptions enough space to be checked. Compress routine steps with adequate justification or precise references. In a talk, present the proof mechanism and move full derivations to backup slides when needed.

## Numerical evidence and presentation

Select the findings that best explain the contribution and demonstrated strengths. Use scientific relevance and consistency across examined settings to guide emphasis. Preserve reversals, uncertainty, and tradeoffs that materially change the conclusion. Use a consistent comparison basis across settings and report only verified numerical comparisons.

Give captions and interpretation complementary roles. Captions describe the figure and the information needed to read it. Main text or spoken-slide takeaways explain what the results establish and why it matters. Avoid repeating axis definitions, panel descriptions, or setup details unless a brief reminder is necessary.

Avoid mechanical narration such as "Three findings stand out", "One finding per setting", or "One xxx per yyy". State the substantive takeaway directly. This does not prohibit precise design facts, numbered proof steps, or concise slide bullets with distinct purposes.

## Direct American English prose

Use American English spelling, such as "analyze" and "modeling", unless the paper's style guide explicitly requires another variety. Preserve exact quotations, bibliographic titles, and code identifiers.

Never use em dashes in authored manuscript prose, including Unicode em dashes, LaTeX `---`, and `\textemdash`. Recast the sentence with commas, a colon, parentheses, or separate sentences. Minimize semicolons: prefer a period or a direct conjunction when it expresses the relationship clearly. Preserve semicolons with mathematical meaning, such as parameter separators in notation.

Prefer direct subjects and verbs. Use "is" or "are" instead of "serves as" or "acts as" when the meaning is simply identity or description. Remove filler such as "it is worth noting that", "it should be emphasized that", and "in order to". Avoid boilerplate emphasis such as "Notably," and "Importantly,". Let the result and its evidence establish its importance.

Remove decorative or generic AI vocabulary: "delve", "underscore", "intricate", "tapestry", "testament", abstract "landscape", "pivotal", "showcase", "foster", filler "leverage", "realm", and "seamless". Replace vague praise with the actual statistical property or finding. Preserve legitimate technical terms, such as leverage scores, when they have a precise mathematical meaning.

Replace vague attributions such as "experts argue" with a specific, verified citation and an accurate account of its claim, or remove the attribution. Avoid negative parallelisms such as "not just X, but Y". State the contribution directly. Remove rule-of-three padding: retain only items with distinct substantive roles. Use a stable term for each referent instead of cycling through synonyms for stylistic variety.

Give each sentence one main idea. Review sentences longer than about 30 words or containing three or more subordinate clauses for opportunities to split or simplify them. Treat these as review signals, not mechanical length limits. Remove subordinate clauses that add no substance, and preserve logical dependencies and qualifications when splitting. Avoid repeated comma-delimited parenthetical phrases. Integrate modifiers into the main clause or use a clear introductory clause.

Keep sentence flow continuous by placing short modifiers next to the words they modify. Avoid detaching an ordinary qualifier as a trailing afterthought or interrupting the main clause with unnecessary pauses. Prefer "Two questions are usually asked of these data together" to "Two questions are asked of these data, usually together." Recast the sentence rather than merely deleting commas, and preserve the modifier's intended scope. Retain punctuation needed for grammar or an essential qualification.

## Final prose pass

Check the central message, claim scope, definitions at first use, useful interpretation, and allocation of detail. For opening material, check that readers understand the problem and its importance before encountering technical machinery. Check American spelling or the required journal variety, remove em dashes, minimize prose semicolons, and simplify stacked clauses and unnecessary pauses. Replace vague attribution and boilerplate emphasis with evidence. Keep terms stable and remove padded lists. These checks supplement the invoking skill's technical audit and never replace it.
