# Academic Presentation Principles

Read alongside [the shared writing principles](writing-principles.md) for Beamer or PowerPoint work. Apply the same precise, natural academic voice used in papers, with shorter text suited to a talk. These rules govern content and layout. The invoking skill supplies format-specific production and validation requirements.

## Narrative and Statistical Content

Read the source material before outlining. Identify the central statistical question, key idea, supported contributions, and evidence. Establish the background and importance before technical machinery. A useful progression is motivation, formulation, opportunity for an advance, method intuition, estimator or test, main guarantee, proof idea, empirical evidence, and implications. Adapt this progression to the audience and material rather than imposing every section.

Determine the audience, talk duration, and any requested slide count. For a 60-minute research talk, roughly 30–35 main slides can be a starting estimate, with backup material separate. Adjust for discussion and mathematical density. Honor an explicit slide count and do not add section dividers or a cover that exceed it.

Give each slide one main purpose. A setup title can name its subject directly, while a results title can state a supported finding. Use short paragraphs, bullets, or fragments as appropriate, with consistent punctuation. Preserve American spelling, direct language, stable terminology, and the shared restrictions on em dashes, filler, and padded lists. Avoid manuscript-length prose and repetitive roadmaps.

Define essential symbols at first use or on an immediately preceding notation slide. Briefly reintroduce symbols after a long gap. State the most useful guarantee with its material conditions and a concise interpretation. Explain what a rate depends on and claim optimality or adaptivity only when supported. Allocate proof space to the decisive idea and difficult transition. Put full derivations and technical variants in backup slides.

Make the estimator or algorithm explicit enough to understand its operation. For a test, show the statistic, calibration, rejection rule, and guarantee. Choose examples that actually exhibit the modeled structure. End with the supported advance, its applicable regime, and the main point the audience should retain.

## Readability and Figure Interpretation

Use consistent typography, alignment, margins, and a restrained academic palette with strong contrast. Keep body text, formulas, plot labels, legends, and captions readable at presentation distance. Avoid decorative elements that compete with the statistical argument. Split a crowded slide or cut secondary material before reducing type size.

Do not place explanatory footnotes in small type below a figure, at the bottom of a slide, or in a tiny strip above the main content. Put a necessary explanation in concise body text or a readable caption. Leave optional narration for the presenter or speaker notes. Keep conditions, uncertainty, and attribution that are essential to understanding the visible claim on the slide in readable text. Use a short author–year citation when appropriate and put full bibliographic detail on reference slides or in notes. Do not hide essential qualifications in notes.

For numerical figures, present the main finding and the method's demonstrated strength without repeating the caption. Captions and legends identify the panels, symbols, and uncertainty displays needed to read the evidence. Preserve material reversals and tradeoffs. Use consistent comparators and metrics across related slides. Tables should be compact and legible, with minimal rules and no decorative grid.

## Final Deck Review

Check the argument and source fidelity, definitions, theorem interpretation, proof emphasis, figure/caption agreement, and visible qualifications. Check that every main slide has one purpose and the deck fits the requested time or count. Render and inspect every slide for clipping, overlap, unreadable text, excessive density, and inconsistent formatting. Fix affected slides and rerender them. Apply the shared prose pass and the invoking skill's format-specific validation before delivery. Report any unperformed validation that materially affects use of the deck.
